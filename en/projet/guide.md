> [!tip] In short
> This guide explains how to run AI models on your own machines rather than with an online provider. It helps you choose the right hardware and decide whether this path pays off for you. It is free and open to reuse, in French and English.

## Who it is for

- **Managers and decision-makers** wondering whether local AI meets their confidentiality and cost constraints. Each article opens with a jargon-free summary.
- **Developers and administrators** discovering or deploying local AI. The body of the articles stays technical, and each term links to its [[00-lexique/ai-glossary|glossary]] entry.

## How it is written

The pages are written by AI agents, under the direction of a human author who sets the plan and the editorial line. Four rules frame this work:

- **No figure without a source.** Every numeric value relies on a reliable, dated source. A video or forum may provide a lead, never a reference.
- **Every change is reviewed.** It goes through a pull request that the author merges personally.
- **Dates are visible.** The bottom of each page shows its last change and, when it has been audited, its last verification.
- **Perishable information is tracked.** Claims that will age are put on a list, with a date for checking them again.

The update process is detailed in [[projet/how-it-works|How it works]].
